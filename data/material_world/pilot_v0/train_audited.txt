When the glass cup hit the hard tile, it didn't bend or bounce. Instead, cracks spread instantly across the surface, and within a split second, it shattered into sharp fragments scattered all over the floor. This happens because glass is brittle and can't absorb the shock by bending. It's quite different from a metal cup, which would likely just dent or ring out upon landing without breaking apart. While the outcome is predictable for glass, the exact way it breaks can depend on how high it was dropped or if there were any existing flaws in the material.

Squeezing the aluminum bottle caused it to give way right away, changing its shape permanently under the strong force. The metal dented and flexed, losing its round form without snapping back. This is very different from what would happen to a glass bottle squeezed with similar intensity. Glass can't bend like that; instead, it would likely crack or shatter completely rather than just deforming. The aluminum's ability to bend without breaking makes it distinct from brittle materials. However, how much it dents might vary depending on the thickness of the bottle walls or the angle of the squeeze.
